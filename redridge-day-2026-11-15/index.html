The Companions
of the Red Hills

From Alther’s Mill and the Shadowhide fires to a hard defeat at Stonewatch: shared courage, names worth remembering and the resolve to try Gath’Ilzogg again today.

CHAPTER 107

The Mill That Had Fallen Silent

The last warmth of Lakeshire stayed in his hands as Xuthar took the eastern road. A few hours earlier those hands had worked cloth and provisions; now they closed around a weapon again. Beyond the settlement, the hills wore the same generous colours they had worn that morning. They gave no warning of how quickly a peaceful view could become a battlefield.

Foreman Oslow’s problem had seemed almost absurd when set beside the greater dangers of Redridge. A bridge needed timber. Timber needed workers. Workers needed to reach Alther’s Mill without being eaten. Somewhere between the first two perfectly reasonable requirements, the tarantulas had imposed their own arrangement.

At the mill, the difficulty acquired legs. Great dark bodies moved between the pale stumps, their shadows splitting into jointed lines over the red earth. The building still stood, but no reassuring rhythm of labour came from it. The woodcutters had yielded their ground to something that would never understand a shipment, a wage or the people waiting for a bridge.

Xuthar slowed. There would be twelve greater tarantulas to bring down and six egg clusters to destroy before the place could be considered safe. He looked from the nearest spider to the gaps between the stumps and chose his approach. The afternoon’s first battle would be fought for the right of ordinary people to return to work.

CHAPTER 108

Between the White Stumps

The first rush came low across the ground. Xuthar gave way a step, just enough to keep the creature before him, and answered with the power he had learned to hold behind his fear. A spider’s body made an ugly sound when it struck the earth. The next was already moving.

The clearings offered little cover worth trusting. Every stump was a blind corner at the wrong height, every stand of grass another place for movement to vanish. He climbed to a rise and studied the mill below. From there the infestation ceased to be a single threat and became a pattern: paths that crossed, pockets of danger, a route that could be opened if he refused to hurry.

He worked through that pattern. He would not mistake an empty stretch of ground for a cleared mill, nor leave the next brood waiting behind him. Egg clusters were part of the task for the same reason the bridge mattered: someone else would have to stand here after the adventurers had gone.

At last Alther’s Mill ceased to be unfinished business in his mind. He had answered Oslow’s request. He could not see the workers returning yet, but he could imagine the first cautious footsteps, the tools lifted again, the timber moving toward Lakeshire. It was a small victory only if one had never needed the road it might restore.

CHAPTER 109

Where the Broken Fences Led

Beyond the mill the ground opened into scattered trees and broken fences. Xuthar followed the worn way between them, grateful for the broad space above his head. He had endured the morning’s cavern; he felt no obligation to miss it. Here at least an enemy had to share the same sky.

There was work left against the Blackrocks, and the signs of it travelled with him: five of the ten battleworn axes required for Blackrock Menace had been gathered. The separate recovery of stolen weapons and Blackrock supplies remained unfinished. He would not let one success persuade him that every danger in Redridge had obligingly ended with it.

Even his provisions told an incomplete story. The goulash ingredients were nearly assembled: the snouts and spider meat were ready, but one last portion of condor meat was still needed. He smiled at the contrast. It was possible to spend an afternoon contending with the enemies of a kingdom and still be detained by dinner.

Then the eastern hills began to reveal their camps. Smoke threaded between the trees. Shapes moved where no farmer should have been waiting. The next part of the road would demand more than a patient hunter of spiders.

CHAPTER 110

The People Who Chose to Stay

Xuthar did not meet an army. He met people: the kind who could have continued along their own roads and instead turned toward the same trouble. Dusty Rune stood with the company; Euphos Galewind moved with the assurance of a hunter, a loyal companion close at hand. Lanai Skystar brought a druid’s strength to their shared advance.

For a moment Xuthar thought of Carro. Not because these travellers could replace him, but because a familiar lesson had returned in unfamiliar faces. Courage changed shape in company. A patch of ground that looked impossible to cross alone became a place where one person could move while another held the danger’s attention.

The Shadowhide camps spread among the red banks and old trees. Tents leaned beside cooking fires, crates and barrels lay within reach, and the gnolls moved with the unsettling confidence of creatures who expected the hillside to remain theirs. Farther above them, stone rose through the foliage like a warning.

They went forward together. No anthem announced the decision. A change of stance, the movement of a companion toward the next enemy, the first answer of magic: these were enough. Xuthar felt the company take hold around him, and found his own place inside it.

CHAPTER 111

Ten Pendants for Lakeshire

Bailiff Conacher’s commission had given a name to the threat: Solomon’s Law. The Shadowhide gnolls served Morganth, an enemy whose influence reached beyond the immediate violence of a camp. Ten pendants were required as proof that the clan’s hold had been challenged. Ten small objects stood between a distant official request and the dangerous business of fulfilling it.

The fighting refused to remain as tidy as the commission. One gnoll pressed in while another broke away; a creature on the rise threatened the space they had just secured. The companions adjusted, advanced, gathered themselves again. Firelight caught the edges of their movement. What had been an enemy camp became a succession of hard-won places to stand.

Xuthar kept his attention on the others as much as on the threat ahead. He had learned that survival could turn on the moment when someone noticed a danger that belonged, for an instant, to somebody else. He answered where he was needed and trusted the others to do the same. The trust was young. It held.

Each pendant recovered gave weight to the work. He did not see them as trophies. They were evidence that this corner of Redridge was contested now, that Lakeshire’s fear had met people willing to walk toward its cause. Around the next fire, another struggle waited. The company moved to meet it.

CHAPTER 112

The Voice Inside the Spoils

The tenth Shadowhide pendant came into Xuthar’s keeping amid the disorder left by battle. There was no silence grand enough for the moment. Enemies still moved among the camps, his companions were still close, and the earth remained littered with the plain consequences of the fight. Yet the long demand of Solomon’s Law had been answered.

Another pendant carried a stranger burden. Its significance drew him away from the simple arithmetic of proof and reward. Theocritus of the Tower of Azora had found a way to send a message through the same devices that linked the Shadowhides to Morganth. An enemy’s instrument had become the vessel for a different voice.

Xuthar knew the Tower of Azora. The thought of that distant place entering the rough immediacy of the hillside unsettled him more than he cared to admit. Here was a thread drawn through places he had believed separate: a mage’s patient research, a gnoll camp, the object resting in his hand. He kept it carefully. Theocritus’ Retrieval would have its place among the day’s accomplishments, and the mystery behind the pendants would not be mistaken for Morganth’s defeat.

He had crossed another threshold himself. Twenty-two measures of the road lay behind him now. He felt it less as triumph than as a steadier knowledge of what he could give to the companions beside him. The man who had left Lakeshire that afternoon was already becoming someone he would have to understand anew.

CHAPTER 113

What Could Be Said Beside a Campfire

When the pressure eased, the small camp seemed almost bewilderingly ordinary. A fire still burned. The trees had not moved. Somewhere above the fighting, the leaves had continued their own quiet business while the company worked to survive below.

Xuthar turned toward the others and thanked them. The words were brief because exhaustion had stripped away the temptation to decorate them. He meant the held ground, the dangers shared, the moments when another’s strength had given him room to use his own. He meant that he had not been alone.

Euphos’s companion remained close, and Lanai’s great animal shape belonged to the scene as naturally as the old tree beside the camp. Dusty Rune’s name joined theirs in Xuthar’s memory. He would remember them as travellers who had chosen the same difficult patch of earth and made it passable together.

There would be other groups, other arrangements of courage, other names to learn before a dangerous crossing. The afternoon had taught him how swiftly strangers could become the people one trusted to watch the next few steps. It did not make farewells easier. It made them matter.

CHAPTER 114

A Bed Beneath the Branches

Later, beside the worn fences, Xuthar made a small place for stillness. The ground was rough, but it was open ground. He could look upward without finding a roof of stone waiting inches above his thoughts. After the long succession of dangers, that was a luxury no innkeeper could have priced.

His bedroll held the shape of a man who had finally admitted the weight in his limbs. The red earth lay close; the forest carried sounds he no longer needed to answer immediately. He let his breathing find its own pace.

The day would not arrange itself into a single victory. There had been the lake, the cavern, two new friends, the crafts practised at Lakeshire; then spiders at the mill, broken fences, gnolls beside their fires and companions whose names had become worth keeping. Some work was done. Some remained. That was how a life on the road advanced, through moments larger than their place on any list.

Xuthar closed his eyes for a little while. He did not surrender the afternoon to forgetting. He set it down carefully, as he might set down a cup too full to carry farther without spilling. Beyond the branches, Redridge waited. For once, he allowed it to wait.

Chapter 115 · The Walls of Stonewatch

The Gate That Would Not Yield

The afternoon’s gratitude had scarcely settled into memory when Stonewatch rose before them. Its walls held the late light without returning any warmth. At the foot of the steps Xuthar paused beside a new company: Azdaha Silvernet, Honkisen Honkzilla, Nethir Rath, and the bear Björn, whose patient strength seemed almost too solid for the uncertain business ahead.

They had come for Gath’Ilzogg. The purpose was simple enough to speak aloud; the road to fulfilling it had already begun to demand more than a name. Blackrock defenders stood between the companions and their quarry. Every piece of ground won on the approach was ground they would need to remember if they were forced to come back the other way.

The entrance waited above its short flight of steps. Beyond the gate, darkness caught between the stones. Xuthar had spent the morning learning how much he disliked enclosed places and the afternoon remembering what company could make possible. Neither lesson cancelled the other. He looked toward the companions, then followed them in.

The hunt was still unfinished, but another task had reached its end: Blackrock Menace. The work against the Blackrocks had not been wasted. Yet the smaller success offered no promise that Stonewatch would surrender its greater prize. They would have to earn every step.

Chapter 116 · The Walls of Stonewatch

The House of Narrow Footsteps

Inside, Stonewatch seemed built to keep a man uncertain of the space around him. Heavy beams pressed across the view. Splintered timber, discarded bones and the scant light of a lantern made each corner a place to study before passing. Xuthar could hear the company more clearly than he could see it.

Honkisen and Björn were close ahead. The bear filled the passage with a reassuring presence, though reassurance could not widen a stair. They moved upward through the cramped interior, where every turn made a clean formation harder to hold and every pause gave exhaustion time to catch them.

The fighting went on. The measure of it stopped being minutes and became the repeated effort of beginning again: gather breath, find the others, answer the next threat, make room where there seemed to be none. Xuthar’s confidence no longer belonged to the easy glow of an afternoon victory. It had become something quieter and more expensive.

At one landing, the lantern showed a wall, a railing, the heavy grain of old wood. Such ordinary things. He found himself noticing them with painful clarity, as if memory had chosen this unremarkable corner to preserve the strain of the whole assault. Somewhere beyond their progress, Gath’Ilzogg remained the reason they had come.

Chapter 117 · The Walls of Stonewatch

When Courage Reached Its Limit

They fought for a long time. Long enough for the first expectation of success to lose its voice; long enough for stubbornness to begin disguising itself as certainty. The companions continued trying to turn the next opening into the one that would finally carry them through.

But the opening did not come. Their effort failed to bring down Gath’Ilzogg. That was the truth the evening placed before them, without kindness and without ornament. No head had been taken, no final triumph earned. The strength they had brought to Stonewatch had met a resistance it could not overcome on this attempt.

Xuthar felt the defeat first as disbelief, then as the dull weight of all the effort that had led nowhere he could point to as victory. He wanted to rearrange the memory into something less painful. He could not. The book he carried would have to make room for this as honestly as it had made room for VanCleef and Bazil Thredd.

Yet the companions’ courage did not vanish because the outcome had disappointed it. Azdaha, Honkisen, Nethir and the others had stayed through the difficult work. Their names belonged beside the attempt, not beneath an excuse for it. They had failed together. It mattered that they had also stood together.

Chapter 118 · The Walls of Stonewatch

The Promise Beneath the Walls

Outside, Redridge had darkened toward evening. The company gathered again on the road below the fortress, where fallen Blackrock defenders marked how far their effort had carried them. Looking upward, Xuthar could still see the walls that had denied them. Looking toward his companions, he could see why the story had not ended there.

There was a difficult mercy in returning to open ground. The broad hillside gave them room to stand apart, to breathe, to collect thoughts that the cramped interior had scattered. Honkisen and Björn remained part of that familiar cluster; Azdaha’s presence beside the road steadied the scene. Nethir’s name stayed with Xuthar as he counted the people who had shared the attempt.

He thought of how readily a victorious afternoon had tempted him to believe the road was beginning to understand him. Stonewatch had corrected that vanity. Growing stronger did not mean that every danger would now become manageable. It meant learning what to do after discovering that one was still not strong enough.

They would try again today. The decision held no guarantee, and perhaps that gave it more weight. They would return carrying the knowledge of this failed assault, the cost of its narrow passages, the memory of where their strength had begun to give. Gath’Ilzogg still lived. Their resolve had survived as well.

Xuthar did not write a victory before it happened. He left the page open, with enough space for another attempt.

Chapter 119 · The Strength to Stand Alone

The Unwelcome Guest

The memory of Stonewatch had not left him. It remained in the heaviness of his shoulders, in the recollection of cramped stairs and effort that had failed to become victory. Xuthar could honour the companions who had stood beside him and still feel the private sting of returning without what they had sought. He carried both feelings onto the next road.

Another unfinished task waited in Redridge. Its modest name, An Unwelcome Guest, made the danger sound almost domestic. Bellygrub was no guest one could dismiss with a firm word at the door. The great boar was the living obstacle between Xuthar and the tusk he had been asked to recover. This time no company gathered around him. He would face the creature alone.

He stopped long enough to let his breathing settle. Defeat could make a man reckless in the effort to contradict it. He knew that temptation now: the wish to force the world to return some certainty before the evening grew older. He would not give Bellygrub that advantage. He needed attention more than anger.

When the struggle began, there was no companion’s movement to follow and no other voice to divide the danger. The creature’s weight and fury belonged entirely to the space before him. Xuthar answered with the strength he had learned along the road, holding his purpose through the moments when courage felt less like confidence than the stubborn refusal to waste the next chance.

He could not set the fight down in somebody else’s hands. Each decision was his; each opening had to be recognised and used. The same solitude that made the danger harsher made every small success unmistakably his own. He kept going until the great boar’s resistance finally ended and the long demand of the hunt was answered.

Bellygrub was defeated. Xuthar had completed An Unwelcome Guest alone. For a moment he stood with the knowledge before attempting to turn it into words. The achievement needed no embellishment. After an evening that had shown him the limits of his strength, he had found a place where that strength was enough.

It did not undo Stonewatch. Gath’Ilzogg still lived, and the companions’ difficult attempt remained part of the day. But failure no longer held the evening’s only answer. Xuthar had learned something he could carry back toward the fortress: a setback could hurt without deciding what came next.

He thought of Carro, of the travellers who had shared the Shadowhide fighting, of those who had pushed through Stonewatch with him. There would be victories to earn beside friends again. This one he would bring to them as his own story. The last witness had stood alone, and this time he had prevailed.
