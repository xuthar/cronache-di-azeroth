THE LAST WITNESS · CHAPTERS 130–139

This is the evening their return became a victory: stolen steel recovered, the fires beneath Stonewatch challenged, an old defeat answered, a warlock’s ambition broken, and the patient protection of Corporal Keeshan on his way out of the cave.

Chapter 130 · Before the Red Hills Trembled

A Table for Two

The cooking fire had reduced the world to a kettle, a wooden table and the welcome weight of another person sitting close enough to hear. Beyond Lakeshire, the mountains held their old dangers. Here, beside the crates and the little flame, Carro Steelcave looked almost offensively comfortable. Broken Tooth lingered nearby with the patient confidence of a creature who expected his share of whatever happened next.

Xuthar set down his pack. Yesterday’s exhaustion had not disappeared; it had become something he could carry again. The difference stood on the other side of the table, checking his gear between moments of apparent idleness.

“We could stay here,” Xuthar suggested.

Carro glanced at the kettle. “We could. Until someone reads your list.”

The list was already out. Its creases divided Redridge into small territories of unfinished obligation. At the top was a request that seemed almost gentle beside the names below it: Redridge Goulash. Chef Breanna’s stores had suffered while she fed the troops during the orc troubles. Five snouts, five portions of condor meat, five helpings of spider meat: even the kitchen had become part of the defence of Lakeshire.

Now that small promise could be answered. The missing ingredient no longer stood between Xuthar and a finished task. He found himself disproportionately pleased by that. A person could grow weary of measuring usefulness in the size of the enemy standing before him.

Carro watched him draw his finger down the page. At Gar’im, the movement slowed. At Gath’Ilzogg, it stopped.

Neither needed the other to explain Stonewatch. The earlier defeat had left Xuthar with a memory of effort spent against something that would not yield. He had honoured those who had struggled beside him. Still, there were evenings when the unfinished name seemed to look back from the paper.

Carro rose, and the bench scraped against the ground. “Come on, priest.”

Xuthar folded the list. The little fire continued its ordinary work behind them as they stepped away. He would remember it later: how quietly an evening could begin before it became one of the nights a friendship would carry for years.

Chapter 131 · The Fire Beneath Stonewatch

The Incinerator’s Broken Promise

The approach narrowed between red stone and dark trees. Ahead, the mouth of the cave gathered the fading light and gave none of it back. Incinerator Gar’im waited there, his name no longer merely a warning upon a notice. Around him lay the strength of the incursion, the shadowcasters whose presence had turned this part of Redridge into a threat to everything below.

The wanted notice had spoken of an alliance with the Blackrock at Stonewatch. Gar’im’s followers could reinforce the keep; the danger at the cave and the danger on the heights belonged to the same tightening fist. Xuthar understood why Solomon needed more than another report that the orcs were dangerous. Lakeshire already knew. What it lacked was room to breathe.

Carro studied the entrance. Broken Tooth’s attention fixed upon the darkness. Xuthar felt the old instinct to count every possible disaster before allowing himself a single step.

“If you discover a pleasant way of doing this,” Carro murmured, “do tell me.”

There was no pleasant way. They went forward.

Fire made a cruel language of the shadows. Rock flashed warm and fell dark again; the familiar outline of his companion appeared, vanished, appeared. Xuthar learned the fight through those brief recognitions: where Carro stood, where Broken Tooth moved, where the next danger might open. He held to that knowledge when the thought of turning back offered itself with sudden, persuasive clarity.

Then the pressure changed. What had seemed an unbroken threat began to give way in moments small enough to miss if he had stopped attending. They stayed with it. Gar’im’s challenge was answered, and the broken staff became the proof that the incursion’s leader could threaten Lakeshire no longer.

But they had come for more than a single trophy. Blackrock Blockade demanded eight stolen weapons and ten stores of supplies from the camps below Stonewatch. Those were the tools by which frightened households became tomorrow’s casualties. Taking them away weakened the hands that meant to use them.

Among tents, timber and the remains of the enemy’s preparations, Xuthar and Carro completed that work as well. Steel could return to the people who needed it; supplies would cease to sustain the advance.

Behind them, the cave remained a black opening in the hillside. Ahead stood Stonewatch. Xuthar adjusted his pack, feeling the strange lightness that follows a fear finally faced. There was still a harder name waiting.

Chapter 132 · The Fortress That Remembered

The Name Beneath the Last Defeat

Stonewatch rose in broken walls and stubborn stone. Xuthar recognised the approach before he wanted to. His body remembered it in advance of his thoughts: the tightening beneath the ribs, the involuntary care with which he placed his feet. Some places made a traveller feel that the last attempt had never ended.

Carro moved beside him. That quiet fact gave the memory a boundary. The earlier company, their courage, that earlier failure belonged to the road behind them. This attempt belonged to the hands they had brought today.

Gath’Ilzogg had led the assault upon the keep and the raids against Lakeshire. His strength reached beyond the room in which a challenger might finally meet him. It lived in every family obliged to wonder whether the next alarm would come too late, in every guard asked to defend more ground than his numbers could hold. Xuthar had carried those fears in other people’s words. Now they travelled into the fortress with him.

The climb demanded attention. There was no room to fight the remembered battle while facing the present one. Xuthar forced himself to notice stone, distance, movement, the sound of Carro still near. Each observation was a small act of rebellion against the certainty that the keep would refuse him again.

And at last the name on the page had nowhere left to hide.

The struggle with Gath’Ilzogg belonged to the fierce, narrow country in which the next decision becomes the whole of a life. Xuthar did not feel like a figure in a song. He felt the effort of staying useful. He felt the trust required to leave one danger to another pair of hands. He felt, with a clarity that frightened him, how much he wanted both of them to walk out.

They endured. The resistance that had once sent him away finally broke.

Gath’Ilzogg was defeated. His head would answer Solomon’s demand for proof. For a moment Xuthar could do nothing with the knowledge except stand inside it. The fortress was still stone; the wind still found the gaps in its walls. Yet a sentence he had carried for days had changed its ending.

“There,” Carro said softly.

Xuthar looked at him. The dwarf had allowed himself one unguarded smile.

The keep had not become harmless, and Redridge had not been freed from every danger. But the man who had returned in defeat was leaving with the task accomplished. He thought of the companions who had tried before, and silently made room for them in the victory.

Then he looked toward the road beyond the walls. He was still alive. Carro was beside him. For once, the name he would write was the enemy’s.

Chapter 133 · The Lion and the Warlock

A Shadow Answered by Its Witness

The old lion stood among the trees as though it had outlasted every explanation for its presence. At its feet, the evening drew inward around a small light. Xuthar stopped there with Carro and Broken Tooth, carrying the heat of recent danger into a stillness that seemed to belong to another age.

Theocritus’ inquiry had travelled a long way to reach this moment. The watchful eye, the patient work of looking further, the warnings carried from Azora: each had drawn attention toward Morganth. Now the threat had a shape larger than one ambitious warlock. Theocritus feared Morganth’s search for the Scythe of Elune, and what such power might mean for Redridge and Stormwind.

Xuthar knew enough of shadows to distrust the comfort of giving them another man’s name. He could not dismiss Morganth as something wholly foreign to himself. They had both reached toward powers that made the devout uneasy. The difference would have to live in what they were willing to do with those powers.

Carro waited until he closed the thought away. “Ready?”

“As much as the question allows.”

Within the Tower of Ilgalar, books crowded the walls above a hollow stair. Warm windows gave the chamber a scholar’s appearance, an indecent civility for the danger gathered beneath them. Shadowhide followers and the warlock’s summoned company occupied the room. Morganth stood ahead, and fire crossed the air.

For one bitter instant Xuthar thought of all the rooms in which knowledge had promised to make the world less frightening. This one had learned to use fear as furniture.

They entered the struggle together. Carro was a solid, familiar presence against the tower’s confusing heights; Broken Tooth brought movement into spaces that seemed to belong entirely to hostile purpose. Xuthar held his will through the pressure, answering the darkness without offering it the right to decide who he would become.

Morganth fell. The Pendant of Shadow, sought as the proof of his defeat, would carry the answer back into Theocritus’ unfinished inquiry. The rumour of the Scythe remained a warning; no legendary weapon lay suddenly in their hands. What they had ended was the immediate threat of the man who meant to find it.

Xuthar looked at the books when the room allowed him breath again. There was something terrible about seeing how ordinary the bindings were.

“Enough reading for one evening?” Carro asked.

Xuthar let out a weary breath that almost became laughter. “I should prefer a book with fewer people trying to kill us.”

The dwarf nodded toward the way down. Together, they left the tower’s knowledge behind and carried its consequence into the open air.

Chapter 134 · Fifteen Against a Township

What the Bounty Could Not Count

The Blackrock camps made the hills look occupied even where no figure moved. Timber, watchfires and the deliberate placement of supplies spoke of an enemy preparing to remain. The countryside was being arranged around the expectation that Lakeshire would eventually give way.

Guard Howe’s demand had sounded severe when Xuthar first heard it: fifteen Blackrock Champions. These were the leaders within the enemy’s units, the strength by which scattered raiders became an organised threat. The number could be put down neatly in a journal. Meeting it would require something less tidy.

Xuthar and Carro pressed onward. Their progress was built from decisions that would never make an impressive song: a pause before advancing, a danger allowed to move past, the discipline to stay within reach. The evening had already demanded much of them. They could not afford to mistake recent victories for an endless supply of strength.

The Champions’ resistance made every careless movement expensive. Xuthar kept finding the next useful thing to do. Beside him Carro did the same, and between them those small acts accumulated into the larger achievement. The bounty was fulfilled. Fifteen of the Blackrock’s hardened leaders would trouble the township no more.

Yet the ground itself seemed determined to conceal another demand. A cave opened beneath the rocks, its entrance crowded by rough stone and pale growths. Beyond it lay the blue-green darkness that Xuthar had learned to distrust.

He stopped. Carro noticed.

“I know,” the dwarf said.

Xuthar looked into the narrow opening. He disliked caves for their patience: how quietly they could close the world down to the distance between a man and the next corner. He had already spent too much of his life discovering how large fear could become in a small place.

But someone had been swallowed by this one.

He could not yet see the prisoner. He did not yet know which voice would ask them to stay. Still, as Carro moved toward the entrance and Broken Tooth followed, Xuthar understood that the work recorded by the bounty had not exhausted what the hills required of them.

He lowered his head beneath the stone and went inside.

Chapter 135 · The Man the Mountain Kept

Forgive My Slow Pace

Inside, the cave held the colour of deep water. Stone descended in teeth from the roof; broad mushrooms crowded the edges of the passage. The air seemed to have forgotten that there was a sky.

Corporal Keeshan waited among those cold walls.

At first Xuthar saw the space around him: how little room there was to move, how far a wounded man would have to travel before the entrance could become a promise. Then the soldier’s presence gathered itself into a person. The task had acquired a face.

Keeshan’s regiment had been overwhelmed. He had survived the blow that took him out of the battle, only to wake in captivity while most of his fellows would never wake again. Xuthar listened without interrupting. There were truths a man could scarcely force through his own mouth; he should not have to compete with a stranger’s questions.

The corporal needed help to reach safety. His injuries made the distance cruel.

“My wounds are grave,” he told Xuthar. A little later came the apology that made the priest’s throat tighten: “Forgive my slow pace.”

Xuthar had expected fear. He had expected pain. He had not expected a badly hurt man, held in a cave by the enemy, to worry that surviving might inconvenience the people who had found him.

Carro’s expression changed. The easy irony with which he had faced Gar’im and Stonewatch went quiet.

“Then slow is how we go,” Xuthar said.

The words were simple enough to be kept. That was their weight. They meant that the two companions could not take the freedom they had won and leave the soldier to discover whether his damaged body could follow. Every hurried advantage would have to be measured against the man behind them.

Broken Tooth stood near the little gathering, fur warm against the cavern’s cold colours. Carro checked the way ahead. Xuthar watched Keeshan gather the strength required to begin.

There would be no grand charge out of the darkness. The first victory was a wounded man managing to stand. The next would be the first steps. After the fallen leaders, the broken staff and the shadowed tower, the evening had entrusted them with something more fragile than proof.

Xuthar moved close enough that Keeshan would not have to wonder where his protectors were. Together they began the slow road out.

Chapter 136 · No One Left in the Dark

The Measure of a Footstep

Water broke the cavern’s silence. Ahead, pale blue light gathered over a pool, and a narrow shelf of earth followed its edge. Keeshan moved with the concentrated care of someone who could not afford to spend strength twice.

Xuthar found himself listening for each step.

In battle he had learned to count openings, distances, the short interval before an enemy could make another choice. Here the measure changed. One foot placed safely. A pause that did not become a collapse. Another breath, another few paces. It was possible to feel the greatness of an undertaking without finding anything swift about it.

Carro kept his attention upon the route. He did not try to fill the silence with encouragement that the corporal would have to find energy to answer. Broken Tooth moved with them, a living reassurance that the company remained whole.

The cave mouth finally admitted the colour of Redridge. Open air touched Xuthar’s face with such ordinary freedom that he nearly stopped. Keeshan had crossed from confinement into the country beyond it. But the entrance was only the first boundary. Enemies still held the ground outside.

Near the rocks, the companions guarded the soldier through the next stretch. The hills had not agreed to spare him merely because the darkness was behind his back. Xuthar would not let the sight of daylight persuade him to relax before safety had become more than a direction.

He had once imagined that heroic lives must be made chiefly from decisive moments. A foe falls; a gate opens; a banner rises. Keeshan’s escape taught him how much could depend upon all the moments between those moments—the uncelebrated work of remaining close when the pace was slow and the road still hostile.

Carro glanced back. Xuthar answered with a small nod. The corporal was with them.

They continued beneath the trees. The red earth was no longer the floor of a battlefield alone; it was the distance a survivor still needed to cross. Protecting that distance became their purpose.

Missing In Action would end as a completed assignment. Xuthar knew how small those words would look when he finally wrote them. He would have to remember the water, the cold, the careful feet. He would have to remember that a man had apologised for being wounded, and that they had stayed.

The great victories could keep their terrible names. This one would keep Keeshan’s.

Chapter 137 · The Hill Beyond the Last Fight

When the Howling Changed

Beyond the cave country, the hills opened into familiar red slopes. The evening still had teeth. Redridge wildlife moved through the narrow ground ahead, and the two companions kept the patient attention that had carried them through the rest of the journey.

Xuthar thought of Yowler.

The hunt for the gnoll leader belonged among the day’s answered demands. Verner Osgood had spoken of the cries above Lakeshire, of one voice stronger than the others, of a pack whose confidence gathered around a dangerous leader. The township’s soldiers were already committed against the orcs. Local fear had been left to find its own defenders.

He and Carro had become part of that answer. Yowler’s Paw marked the end of the hunt; Howling in the Hills could pass out of the list of dangers still waiting.

The achievement had a different weight after Keeshan. Xuthar could imagine how a sound travelled into a town at night: heard through a shutter, across a yard, by someone trying to persuade a child that the door would hold. He could imagine how much courage ordinary people spent upon dangers they never met face to face.

Perhaps no one would notice, all at once, what had changed. A quieter stretch of road might remain merely a road. A household spared trouble might never know whose hands had kept it away. That was the kind of victory his book had been meant to hold from the beginning.

Carro interrupted his thoughts by shifting his gear with a faint, dissatisfied grunt.

“What?” Xuthar asked.

“Trying to decide which part of me will complain first tomorrow.”

Xuthar looked at the red slope ahead, then at his friend. After all the names they had faced, that modest problem seemed almost miraculous.

“Let them take turns.”

“They’ll never agree.”

Their laughter travelled only a little way between the trees. Beyond it lay the memory of the hunt, the risk of the road, the work already finished. Xuthar let the laughter remain part of the record.

Chapter 138 · Above the Roofs of Home

Let the Living Have the View

At last the two companions stood above the roofs, with the red mountains gathered in the distance. Leaves crowded the edges of the view. A pointed roof rose between them and the settlement below, modest and familiar after the towering walls they had entered.

Broken Tooth stood beside Carro. Xuthar looked from one to the other and felt the simple wonder of an evening that had brought them all this far.

Gar’im’s staff. Gath’Ilzogg’s head. Morganth’s pendant. Stolen weapons and supplies recovered, Champions defeated, Yowler’s hunt answered, Keeshan brought through the darkness. Each achievement had seemed large enough to command the whole day when it was still waiting ahead. Together they now formed a road that Xuthar could scarcely believe they had walked.

He had returned to Redridge carrying an old defeat. He was leaving this height with the knowledge that defeat had been a page, not the binding of his life.

Carro watched the country below. For once neither man hurried to make the silence entertaining. They had earned a moment in which the view could belong to them.

Xuthar thought of another kind of courage written among his completed obligations. Messenger to Darkshire carried Solomon’s appeal toward Lord Ebonlocke and the Night Watch: Lakeshire asking another troubled town to spare help. Even in victory, the request mattered. A township could not build a lasting defence from the hope that two travellers would always be near when the next threat arrived.

He would remember the letter’s purpose beside the battles. He would remember the kitchen emptied while feeding soldiers. Redridge’s needs had been held together by people working far from the grandest danger, each keeping some essential corner of the world from falling apart.

“What will you call it?” Carro asked.

Xuthar knew which it the dwarf meant. He considered the keep, the warlock’s chamber, the green darkness and the slow steps beside the pool.

“I don’t know yet.”

Carro nodded. “Good. Means you’re still looking.”

Xuthar smiled. The book could wait a little longer for its title.

For now, the living had the view. The priest, the dwarf and the loyal hunter’s companion stood beneath the trees while Lakeshire lay below them. After everything the evening had demanded, they were allowed this ordinary, enormous thing: to be there together.

Chapter 139 · What a Victory Must Remember

The Names Beside His Own

When Xuthar finally opened the journal, he did not begin with the enemies.

He wrote Carro Steelcave. Beneath it, Broken Tooth. Then Corporal Keeshan.

Only after those names were safely upon the page did he turn to the record of the work. Redridge Goulash. Messenger to Darkshire. Blackrock Blockade. Howling in the Hills. Blackrock Bounty. Missing In Action. The two wanted notices, and Morganth. Nine Redridge obligations could stand among the completed lines.

He allowed himself to look at them. There were days when a traveller had to work hard to find a single thing that had gone well. This evening had given him more than he could comfortably gather into one feeling. Pride, relief, gratitude and the delayed trembling of danger all arrived together.

Other completed concerns still accompanied those victories. Business in Auberdine held the worry of Borgus Steelhand for his brother Gorbold and the life of a harbour far away. The Corruption Abroad carried the Nightwhispers’ appeal toward Darkshore, where trouble required hands willing to help. Tinkmaster Overspark recalled the fate of Techbot, a useful creation gone wrong and a maker unwilling to abandon the hope of repair.

The world had more ways to need a traveller than he could count. He wanted his book to keep that breadth. A brother’s unanswered news, a damaged machine, a soldier missing beneath a mountain: each began when somebody refused to accept absence as the end of a story.

Two demands remained plainly open. Shoni’s gyrodrillmatic machines still needed twenty-four sets of mechanical innards; his progress toward them was empty. Collin Mauren still waited for eight crystallized scales, the basilisk reagent sought from Stonetalon. Xuthar left both lines unfinished. Tonight’s victories had earned their truth. They did not need the future borrowed to make them larger.

He turned back to the names at the top of the page. Carro would doubtless find the arrangement too solemn if he saw it, and then ask whether Broken Tooth should have been listed first. Xuthar could almost hear the objection.

The thought made him laugh quietly.

He had begun this journey fearing that memory would make him the last man standing among the names of the lost. This evening had given him living names to write: a friend beside him, a loyal creature near, a wounded soldier carried back into the world by the patience of his protectors.

He put the final mark beside the day’s victories and rested his hand upon the page.

The red hills had asked whether they would return. Together, they had answered.
